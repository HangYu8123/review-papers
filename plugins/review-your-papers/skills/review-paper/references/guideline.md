1. Follow the structure:
summary, then strengths, then weaknesses, then areas for improvement, and finally, overall recommendation.
2. For the summary, summarize the work, but do not always follow the claimed contributions. Summarize the contributions from a fair perspective, then summarize the work, key contributions, key novelties, and slightly mention experiments (unless they are a key contribution), with a positive, compromising, and considerable tone.
3. For strengths, highlight the novelty, what is new, contributions, how this paper can benefit the community, and other strengths.
4. For weaknesses, point out the weaknesses in a nice tone. Ignore the minor weaknesses. Do not have more than two points saying things like: more baselines, more recent work. Focus on what is really insufficient in the work, such as: unclear contributions, insufficient evidence for claimed contributions, wrong/super-outdated methods, etc.
5. Areas for improvement: indicate what can help the paper to improve or get accepted with a reasonable workload. With a nice tone, focus on what can help the paper to improve. This is different from weaknesses: weaknesses are things that have not been done or are wrong and insufficient, but this one is about doing what can make the paper better. One or two points (decent and fair) are sufficient for this.
6. Overall recommendation: provide overall recommendations; reference previous overall recommendations for template reference. This is the usual standard; just create a similar one, slightly summarize the review, explain the recommendation in one short sentence, and say I am not an expert, so I would not argue if I do not largely disagree (like in the template).

7. State machine of the paper (drawn after the review, details at the top of review_template/review.html): map the argument of the paper, not its pipeline. Focus on (1) the key intuition and the key contributions, judged fairly; (2) where the actual work for each contribution is, meaning which modules, derivations, systems, or datasets carry it and where they are in the paper; (3) what experiments were run, with the environment and the total workload, and which key contributions each one supports, marking weak or partial support; (4) other components only when they help to understand the paper or reveal an important weakness.

8. Keep the summary short, usually 80 to 140 words in total. Its second paragraph, if needed, should be one or two sentences on the evaluation and main result. Keep strengths to one or two sentences with one supporting fact. Put detailed table values, equation checks, and calculations in the evidence.
9. Use a professional and restrained tone. Prefer the authors, the method, or the results as the subject. Keep first person for personal concerns and the recommendation, including the expertise caveat. Avoid conversational hedges such as "As far as I know" and accounts of the reviewing process such as "I worked through the equations".
