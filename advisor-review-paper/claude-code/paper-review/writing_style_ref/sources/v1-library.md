# Writing-style library | initial version

Prepared 12 October 2026. This is a **proposed reusable library**, ready for accept/reject review. Its purpose is to support future drafting and rewriting, rather than to copyedit the dissertation in bulk. The earlier failed review is not an approved source; v2 supplies the intended six areas, while the manuscript evidence is checked afresh.

The working voice is practical, explanatory, and explicitly connected: concrete human or robot actions establish the problem; contrasts distinguish the information in teaching signals; an intuition motivates a mechanism; results lead to bounded interpretation. This description is an inference from the selected excerpts, not a frequency claim or proof of individual authorship in coauthored manuscripts.

## How to use the library

1. Identify the rhetorical job: motivate, define, compare, describe a procedure, report evidence, interpret, or bridge to the next method.
2. Select a vocabulary or wording entry, then a sentence or story pattern suited to that job.
3. Fill the slots using the current study's facts. The forms below are reusable skeletons, not scientific claims or text to paste unchanged.
4. Preserve signal distinctions, quantities, citations, conditions, and uncertainty. Repeated technical nouns are often useful.
5. Review and accept the library entry independently of any dissertation example. An accepted entry becomes a future writing preference; it does not authorize changing every matching sentence.

## Vocabulary

### LIB-VOC-01 | Research verbs paired with their operations

**Suggestion type:** reusable library entry. **Status:** proposed; not approved.

**Dissertation location:** not applicable to this reusable entry. Any concrete text revision is listed separately in the application examples below.

**Observation from the papers:** CHARM names study design and investigation; Progress names characterization and testing; Sidetracks names exploratory analysis. These are distinct operations.

**Proposed library wording (revised/reference version):**

Choose the verb by the work performed: investigate or examine a question, characterize a signal or pattern, test or evaluate an empirical outcome, design an instrument or procedure, and analyze collected data.

**Reusable forms / phrase bank:**

- investigate how [human factor] is associated with [teaching pattern]
- characterize [signal / behavior / distribution]
- test [signal] in [setting]
- design a study to investigate [question]
- design a questionnaire to capture [characteristics]
- conduct an exploratory analysis of [behaviors]
- evaluate [method] on [tasks]
- analyze [relationship / trajectories / annotations]

**Use when:** Introducing a study, stating a contribution, or describing a procedure.

**Meaning and scope boundary:** The expanded collocations are proposed forms, not a frequency ranking or a claim that every form occurs verbatim. Investigate does not establish an effect; evaluate does not state the outcome.

**Less suitable in this context:**

- Using demonstrate for an open investigation.
- Using characterize for a performance comparison alone.
- Adding novel or effective when the operation and object already identify the contribution.

**Evidence status:** observed across papers. The proposed wording and slot templates are editorial synthesis; source excerpts are verbatim.

**Original paper wording and exact locations:**

- **CHARM**: `reference_papers/CHARM/ROMAN 2025.tex:54`; Abstract.

```latex
In this work, we designed an exploratory study investigating how human feedback patterns are associated with human characteristics.
```

- **Progress**: `reference_papers/Progress/Ro-MAN 2024.tex:93`; Abstract.

```latex
In this work, we characterize and test a little-used teaching signal: \textit{progress},  designed to represent the completion percentage of a task.
```

- **Demonstration Sidetracks**: `reference_papers/Demonstration_sidetracks/main.tex:177`; Methodology.

```latex

```

### LIB-VOC-02 | Signal names and the quantity each signal describes

**Suggestion type:** reusable library entry. **Status:** proposed; not approved.

**Dissertation location:** not applicable to this reusable entry. Any concrete text revision is listed separately in the application examples below.

**Observation from the papers:** STEADY distinguishes binary and scalar evaluative feedback through response values. PHIRL defines progress by task completion and describes reward alignment against annotations.

**Proposed library wording (revised/reference version):**

Maintain a term-to-quantity bank: binary evaluative feedback expresses a two-way evaluation; scalar evaluative feedback expresses evaluation on a scale; progress describes cumulative task completion; a progress annotation is a supplied label; a reward belongs to the learning formulation; a policy governs action choice. Reuse the technical noun where variation would blur the distinction.

**Reusable forms / phrase bank:**

- binary evaluative feedback: a good/bad evaluation
- scalar evaluative feedback: an evaluation on a scale
- progress: the cumulative degree of task completion
- progress annotations on [demonstrations / states / segments]
- feedback values provided by participants
- a reward function inferred from demonstrations
- a policy learned from [teaching information]
- align learned rewards with progress annotations

**Use when:** Defining modalities or moving between human annotations and learning quantities.

**Meaning and scope boundary:** Numerical representation does not make quantities equivalent. Preserve chapter definitions and notation. An annotation can be noisy even when the intended progress quantity is formally defined. Shorten to feedback or the signal only with an unambiguous referent.

**Less suitable in this context:**

- Substituting reward for feedback for stylistic variety.
- Calling every numerical feedback value progress.
- Using label, annotation, signal, and learned reward as interchangeable nouns when their roles differ.

**Evidence status:** observed across papers. The proposed wording and slot templates are editorial synthesis; source excerpts are verbatim.

**Original paper wording and exact locations:**

- **STEADY**: `reference_papers/STEADY/IEEEexample.tex:144`; Introduction.

```latex
Evaluative feedback is natural for non-experts to provide, and can take the form of either \emph{binary} feedback, in which feedback can only be either ``good'' or ``bad'', or \emph{scalar} feedback, which takes a value from a scale of values (e.g., ``1-10'', ``A-F'', or ``0-5 stars'').
```

- **PHIRL**: `reference_papers/PHIRL/methodology.tex:17`; Methodology > Progress.

```latex
\textit{Progress} is a signal that describes the cumulative degree of task completion, ranging from fully incomplete to fully complete.
```

- **PHIRL**: `reference_papers/PHIRL/methodology.tex:87`; Methodology > Reward Function Shaping.

```latex
We improve the learned reward function by shaping it to align with \textit{progress} annotations during reward learning.
```

### LIB-VOC-03 | A vocabulary for structured imperfections

**Suggestion type:** reusable library entry. **Status:** proposed; not approved.

**Dissertation location:** not applicable to this reusable entry. Any concrete text revision is listed separately in the application examples below.

**Observation from the papers:** Progress describes demonstrations as noisy and multi-policy while still successful. Sidetracks describes non-optimal behavior as structured and names accommodation behaviors alongside noisy actions.

**Proposed library wording (revised/reference version):**

Use imperfections as a broad category, then specify the property: sub-optimal for departure from an optimal trajectory or policy; noisy for the relevant variability or errors; unsuccessful for failure to complete the task; patterned or task-related for temporal/spatial structure; accommodation behavior for the manuscript's account of responding to control limitations. Describe the behavior rather than assigning an undifferentiated deficit to the teacher.

**Reusable forms / phrase bank:**

- sub-optimal but successful demonstrations
- noisy, multi-policy demonstrations
- patterned imperfections in human teaching
- task-related temporal and spatial patterns
- non-optimal behaviors near [task phase changes / targets]
- accommodation behaviors during teleoperation
- redundant but harmless behaviors
- imperfect demonstrations that still complete the task

**Use when:** Discussing teaching data, comparing human demonstrations with simulated noise, or explaining a behavior taxonomy.

**Meaning and scope boundary:** Descriptors need an operational definition or explanatory context. Patterned behavior is not automatically intentional, beneficial, or caused by a named factor. Non-expert describes expertise in the study task.

**Less suitable in this context:**

- Treating every sub-optimal action as a failed demonstration.
- Replacing patterned imperfections with random noise when structure is the result.
- Calling behavior meaningless merely because it does not directly advance completion.

**Evidence status:** observed across papers. The proposed wording and slot templates are editorial synthesis; source excerpts are verbatim.

**Original paper wording and exact locations:**

- **Progress**: `reference_papers/Progress/Ro-MAN 2024.tex:126`; Introduction.

```latex
This is especially true for non-expert demonstrations: non-expert demonstrations can be noisy, multi-policy, and yet still succeed (we show this in \autoref{sectionVC}).
```

- **Demonstration Sidetracks**: `reference_papers/Demonstration_sidetracks/main.tex:180`; Methodology.

```latex
Non-optimal behaviors in human demonstrations are structured with patterns.
```

- **Demonstration Sidetracks**: `reference_papers/Demonstration_sidetracks/main.tex:192`; Methodology > .

### LIB-VOC-04 | Mechanism verbs with distinct inputs and outputs

**Suggestion type:** reusable library entry. **Status:** proposed; not approved.

**Dissertation location:** not applicable to this reusable entry. Any concrete text revision is listed separately in the application examples below.

**Observation from the papers:** CHARM predicts feedback values from characteristics and statistics. PHIRL infers a reward function, calculates rewards, and aligns them with annotations. STEADY reconstructs distributions and rescales feedback.

**Proposed library wording (revised/reference version):**

Tie each verb to its object: predict human-feedback values; infer a latent reward function; calculate rewards from a learned model; shape a reward function through stated constraints; align rewards with annotation information; reconstruct modeled distributions; rescale feedback using its statistics. Use a mechanism verb where improve or enable leaves the change unclear.

**Reusable forms / phrase bank:**

- predict feedback values using [inputs]
- infer a reward function from demonstrations
- calculate rewards over annotated demonstrations
- shape the learned reward function
- align learned rewards with progress annotations
- reconstruct positive and negative feedback distributions
- rescale scalar feedback based on feedback statistics
- combine demonstrations and feedback to learn [quantity]

**Use when:** Describing an algorithm or explaining its computation.

**Meaning and scope boundary:** These are manuscript operations, not universal terminology distinctions. Preserve the implemented object and optimization goal. The recommended spellings reconstruct and rescale normalize source hyphenation.

**Less suitable in this context:**

- Using predict and infer interchangeably when targets differ.
- Saying the method improves feedback without naming the operation.
- Using align to imply numerical identity between rewards and annotations.

**Evidence status:** observed across papers. The proposed wording and slot templates are editorial synthesis; source excerpts are verbatim.

**Original paper wording and exact locations:**

- **CHARM**: `reference_papers/CHARM/ROMAN 2025.tex:425`; Results > Including Human Characteristics Improves Feedback Prediction Accuracy.

```latex
Human characteristics and task statistics (CHARM): the model was trained to predict feedback values using both corresponding task statistics and human characteristics.
```

- **PHIRL**: `reference_papers/PHIRL/CoRL_2026_main.tex:71`; Abstract.

```latex
PHIRL iteratively infers a reward function from demonstrations via inverse reinforcement learning, calculates the learned rewards over the \textit{progress}-annotated demonstrations, and aligns the rewards with \textit{progress} annotations over four dimensions.
```

- **STEADY**: `reference_papers/STEADY/IEEEexample.tex:131`; Abstract.

```latex

```

### LIB-VOC-05 | Information dimensions rather than undifferentiated richness

**Suggestion type:** reusable library entry. **Status:** proposed; not approved.

**Dissertation location:** not applicable to this reusable entry. Any concrete text revision is listed separately in the application examples below.

**Observation from the papers:** Progress pairs dense/global demonstration information with sparse/local feedback. PHIRL pairs policy-level information with local critiques.

**Proposed library wording (revised/reference version):**

Use information nouns with a named dimension: dense policy-level information, sparse local evaluations, global information about goals, local action critiques, direct policy guidance, and complementary information. Explain what two signals add rather than calling them both rich or informative.

**Reusable forms / phrase bank:**

- dense policy-level information
- sparse local evaluations
- global information about policies and goals
- local critiques of robot actions
- direct policy guidance
- complementary information from demonstrations and feedback
- information about [completion / ranking / magnitude]
- [signal A] provides [dimension A], while [signal B] provides [dimension B]

**Use when:** Motivating combined modalities or explaining what a signal adds.

**Meaning and scope boundary:** Density, scope, precision, and accuracy are separate dimensions. Carry qualifications and task context. Direct policy guidance differs from a reward or evaluative annotation.

**Less suitable in this context:**

- Using dense as a synonym for more accurate.
- Using complementary as a substitute for a demonstrated learning benefit.
- Treating properties in one modality comparison as universal.

**Evidence status:** observed across papers. The proposed wording and slot templates are editorial synthesis; source excerpts are verbatim.

**Original paper wording and exact locations:**

- **Progress**: `reference_papers/Progress/Ro-MAN 2024.tex:114`; Introduction.

```latex
Human feedback and human demonstrations can be complementary due to the difference in human knowledge they carry.
```

- **Progress**: `reference_papers/Progress/Ro-MAN 2024.tex:115`; Introduction.

```latex
Demonstrations carry relatively dense and global information including policies and goals, and tend to be less accurate \cite{palan2019learning}.
```

- **PHIRL**: `reference_papers/PHIRL/CoRL_2026_main.tex:68`; Abstract.

```latex
Human demonstrations provide dense policy-level information but sometimes lack local precision. Human feedback presents accurate local critiques, but offers sparse evaluations rather than direct policy guidance.
```

### LIB-VOC-06 | Accuracy, consistency, and robustness with named dimensions

**Suggestion type:** reusable library entry. **Status:** proposed; not approved.

**Dissertation location:** not applicable to this reusable entry. Any concrete text revision is listed separately in the application examples below.

**Observation from the papers:** STEADY discusses consistency when the same participant labels the same data; Progress discusses consistency across users and robustness to unproductive behavior; CHARM distinguishes direction accuracy; PHIRL names robustness to exploitation.

**Proposed library wording (revised/reference version):**

Use dimension-specific collocations: within-participant consistency, consistency across participants, feedback direction accuracy, numerical precision of feedback values, robustness to sub-optimal demonstrations, and robustness to reward hacking. State the operational measure nearby and preserve the dimension when shortening a sentence.

**Reusable forms / phrase bank:**

- consistency within a participant
- consistency across users
- accuracy in feedback direction
- difference between feedback and [reference values]
- numerical precision of feedback values
- robustness to unproductive behaviors
- robustness to reward hacking
- higher [named metric] under [condition]

**Use when:** Reporting evaluation outcomes or synthesizing what the studies establish.

**Meaning and scope boundary:** This taxonomy is a proposed synthesis of source uses. Ratings can be consistent and systematically inaccurate; directional accuracy need not imply numerical precision. An observed reward-hacking scenario does not establish resistance to every exploitation.

**Less suitable in this context:**

- Calling a signal accurate because it is consistent.
- Calling a method robust without naming the disturbance or failure mode.
- Replacing a named metric with better quality or superior performance.

**Evidence status:** proposed synthesis. The proposed wording and slot templates are editorial synthesis; source excerpts are verbatim.

**Original paper wording and exact locations:**

## Writing style

### LIB-STY-01 | Practical purpose before abstract importance

**Suggestion type:** reusable library entry. **Status:** proposed; not approved.

**Dissertation location:** not applicable to this reusable entry. Any concrete text revision is listed separately in the application examples below.

**Observation from the papers:** The prospectus connects adaptation to everyday deployment, and Progress connects its goal to improving learning through a teaching signal. The register is practical and explanatory: the importance of the research is grounded in a task, a person, or a learning requirement.

**Proposed library wording (revised/reference version):**

Make the practical requirement the organizing claim of a motivation paragraph. Follow it with the obstacle and the research response. Use a broad importance statement only when it adds context that the concrete requirement cannot provide.

**Reusable forms / phrase bank:**

- To [deployment or interaction goal], robots need to [capability].
- [Teaching source] makes [practical benefit] possible, but [specific difficulty] limits [learning outcome].
- The goal of [chapter or method] is to [research action] so that [practical learning purpose].
- Motivation paragraph: practical goal → available human teaching → concrete difficulty → question addressed here.

**Use when:** An introduction, chapter opening, motivation paragraph, or contribution statement needs to explain why the work matters.

**Meaning and scope boundary:** This is a register preference, not a requirement that every paragraph start with a purpose clause. Keep theoretical motivation when the chapter's contribution is theoretical.

**Less suitable in this context:**

- An opening that repeats the importance of adaptation without naming what the robot must learn or how users teach it.
- Replacing a qualified practical benefit with an unconditional promise of real-world success.
- Adding generic importance adjectives when the task and obstacle already establish the motivation.

**Evidence status:** observed across papers. The proposed wording and slot templates are editorial synthesis; source excerpts are verbatim.

**Original paper wording and exact locations:**

- **RSS Pioneers prospectus**: `reference_papers/prospectus/RSS Pioneers 2026.tex:36`; Introduction.

```latex
To integrate robots into our everyday lives, it is essential for them to learn and adapt to novices.
```

- **Progress**: `reference_papers/Progress/Ro-MAN 2024.tex:211`; Progress.

```latex
Our goal is to improve learning from human feedback with a new teaching signal: progress.
```

### LIB-STY-02 | Visible research actions and subjects that match the rhetorical level

**Suggestion type:** reusable library entry. **Status:** proposed; not approved.

**Dissertation location:** not applicable to this reusable entry. Any concrete text revision is listed separately in the application examples below.

**Observation from the papers:** The papers use author-present research actions such as designing and conducting a study, while algorithm descriptions use the method name as subject. This keeps research decisions and computational operations visible rather than hiding them inside abstract nouns.

**Proposed library wording (revised/reference version):**

Choose subjects by the sentence's job: this dissertation for the research program, this chapter for local purpose, researchers or we for a consequential study decision, participants for teaching behavior, and a method name for algorithm operations. Preserve the surrounding dissertation voice rather than imposing first person throughout.

**Reusable forms / phrase bank:**

- This dissertation investigates [overarching question] through [linked research approach].
- This chapter examines whether [specific hypothesis or question].
- Participants [teaching action] while [task or condition].
- [Method] [operation] using [information], then [next operation].
- We chose [study design] to [reason], when the researcher decision needs to be explicit and first person fits the local voice.

**Use when:** Describing study aims, design choices, participant procedures, algorithm steps, or chapter contributions.

**Meaning and scope boundary:** The coauthored papers establish manuscript practice, not a single author's personal preference. Passive voice remains useful when the object or procedure is the reader's focus.

**Less suitable in this context:**

- Making a learning framework the subject of a claim about what the researchers observed.
- Attributing data collection or reasoning to an abstract result.
- Changing all passive constructions or all first-person sentences mechanically.

**Evidence status:** observed across papers. The proposed wording and slot templates are editorial synthesis; source excerpts are verbatim.

### LIB-STY-03 | Human-aware diagnosis of teaching imperfections

**Suggestion type:** reusable library entry. **Status:** proposed; not approved.

**Dissertation location:** not applicable to this reusable entry. Any concrete text revision is listed separately in the application examples below.

**Observation from the papers:** The prospectus treats consistently high-quality novice teaching as an impractical expectation. Sidetracks explains behavior through the control interface, reaction time, and perception accuracy, and describes accommodation behaviors that can help the person complete the task. Imperfection is an object of study, not a judgment of the teacher's worth.

**Proposed library wording (revised/reference version):**

Describe the signal or action precisely, then explain the relevant human or interface constraint. Distinguish an inefficient but useful accommodation from a harmful error when evidence permits. Keep expectations about non-experts tied to what the task asks them to do.

**Reusable forms / phrase bank:**

- When [interaction demand], users may [observable behavior] because [supported constraint].
- [Behavior] is inefficient relative to [reference], but can help users [practical purpose].
- The difficulty lies in [teaching requirement or interface demand], which makes [kind of variation] likely.
- Diagnostic paragraph: teaching requirement → observed behavior → relevant constraint → consequence for interpreting the data.

**Use when:** Explaining non-expert demonstrations, feedback variation, interface effects, accommodation behaviors, or noisy teaching data.

**Meaning and scope boundary:** Constructive explanation does not require excusing harmful errors or inferring intent from behavior alone. State whether a mechanism was measured, interpreted, or hypothesized.

**Less suitable in this context:**

- Calling a person unreliable when the measured property belongs to feedback or demonstrations.
- Treating every inefficient action as a mistake.
- Presenting a plausible mental-workload explanation as a measured cause.

**Evidence status:** observed across papers. The proposed wording and slot templates are editorial synthesis; source excerpts are verbatim.

**Original paper wording and exact locations:**

- **RSS Pioneers prospectus**: `reference_papers/prospectus/RSS Pioneers 2026.tex:26`; Abstract.

```latex
However, expecting all of the users, especially those with limited experience with robots, to always provide high-quality teaching is impractical.
```

- **Demonstration Sidetracks**: `reference_papers/Demonstration_sidetracks/main.tex:188`; Methodology > .

```latex
However, unlike trained agents or oracles, which can control all action dimensions instantly and are either provided with accurate observation or trained to reduce perception error, humans are limited by the control interface, reaction time, and perception accuracy.
```

### LIB-STY-04 | Confidence through an inspectable mechanism

**Suggestion type:** reusable library entry. **Status:** proposed; not approved.

**Dissertation location:** not applicable to this reusable entry. Any concrete text revision is listed separately in the application examples below.

**Observation from the papers:** STEADY describes a multi-distributional model, confidence scores, and recovered labels. PHIRL states an intuition about consistency, then names the operations and alignment dimensions. These explanations make the contribution understandable through its mechanism rather than through novelty or effectiveness alone.

**Proposed library wording (revised/reference version):**

Pair a concise intuition with a concrete account of input, operation, and output. Put evaluation claims in a later sentence with their task and comparison. A method description can be confident about what it computes while remaining qualified about what its evaluation establishes.

**Reusable forms / phrase bank:**

- The intuition is that [plain-language relationship between known quantities].
- [Method] transforms [input] into [output] by [operation].
- This makes [information] available to [learner or downstream operation].
- Mechanism paragraph: learning difficulty → intuition → input and transformation → expected relevance → evaluation question.

**Use when:** Introducing an algorithm, explaining a design choice, or stating a methodological contribution.

**Meaning and scope boundary:** Mechanism and benefit are different claims. Explain the computation directly; support claims about improved performance with the relevant evaluation.

**Less suitable in this context:**

- Using effective, novel, or robust as the only explanation of the method's contribution.
- Moving an observed benefit into the intuition as if it were guaranteed by design.
- Using an everyday analogy without returning to the actual computation.

**Evidence status:** observed across papers. The proposed wording and slot templates are editorial synthesis; source excerpts are verbatim.

**Original paper wording and exact locations:**

- **STEADY**: `reference_papers/STEADY/IEEEexample.tex:196`; Introduction.

### LIB-NAR-05 | A results paragraph answers one comparison

**Suggestion type:** reusable library entry. **Status:** proposed; not approved.

**Dissertation location:** not applicable to this reusable entry. Any concrete text revision is listed separately in the application examples below.

**Observation from the papers:** CHARM reports feedback-prediction results separately under five-point and binary settings, gives the performance of the task-statistics model and CHARM, and then interprets what including human characteristics may add. The paragraph develops one comparison before moving to the next scale.

**Proposed library wording (revised/reference version):**

Introduce the evaluation condition, state the paired outcomes, report relevant uncertainty or significance, and explain the bounded answer to the research question. Keep a later causal explanation distinct from the comparison itself.

**Reusable forms / phrase bank:**

- Under [condition], [method A] achieved [measure A], compared with [measure B] for [method B].
- The difference was [uncertainty or significance statement, when available].
- These results [support or suggest] [bounded answer to the comparison question].
- Results paragraph: condition and metric → paired outcomes → uncertainty or statistical evidence → interpretation → scope if needed.
- Multiple-condition section: repeat the same comparison frame for each condition, then synthesize what changes across conditions.

**Use when:** Reporting baseline comparisons, feedback-scale comparisons, ablations, or results under dataset-quality conditions.

**Meaning and scope boundary:** The paragraph shape is portable; the measures, statistics, and scope are not. Retain the current dissertation's facts rather than importing older paper numbers.

**Less suitable in this context:**

- Combining accuracy, return, and success into a general superiority claim without distinguishing the metrics.
- Dropping a baseline or condition when shortening a result sentence.
- Treating a statistically significant difference as a causal explanation of why the method works.

### LIB-NAR-07 | Synthesis explains what becomes clearer together
