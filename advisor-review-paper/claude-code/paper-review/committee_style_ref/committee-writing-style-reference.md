# Committee writing style reference

This reference describes how three dissertations are written. They are by Elaine Schaertl Short (University of Southern California, 2017), Marynel Vázquez (Carnegie Mellon University, 2017), and Andreea Bobu (University of California, Berkeley, 2023). Hang named them as the sources for the committee writing style, and Short is Hang's adviser. The reference shows what these readers did in their own dissertations, which is a guide to what they will find familiar in one.

Use it together with [Hang's writing style reference](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md). Hang's reference says how Hang writes. This one says what the three did, where they agree with one another, and where they differ. When the two references differ, Hang's instructions and Hang's reference take priority. Each pattern has a line on how it relates to Hang's dissertation.

A dissertation shows how its author wrote at that time, with that adviser and those coauthors. It does not state what the author asks of a student now. The patterns are observations, and the forms are reusable wording. Square brackets mark slots to fill. Quotations keep the source wording, including first person, spelling, and punctuation. They are evidence of a pattern and not text to copy.

The reference has 63 patterns and 282 quotations, each checked against its page. Page numbers are the printed page, followed by the page of the PDF file.

## Quick reference

These are the practices that all three share.

1. State the thesis in one sentence and repeat that sentence in the abstract, the introduction, and the conclusion.
2. Name the gap precisely. Say what prior work covers, what it leaves out, and why.
3. Show a problem in a short scene before stating it in general terms.
4. Open each chapter with what it presents and what was found, and say how it follows from the chapter before.
5. Report every study in the same order, with questions or hypotheses first and the verdict attached to the result that decides it.
6. Give a result with its values and its test in one sentence, and keep the interpretation in a hedged clause after it.
7. Report a difference that was not found as not found. Do not call the conditions equal.
8. Offer explanations for an unexpected result as possibilities.
9. Grade the strength of evidence in words, such as preliminary, limited, some support, and a trend, with the value beside the word.
10. State limitations plainly and tie each one to the design of the study.
11. Derive future work from the scope and limits of the dissertation, with a modal that shows how firm the plan is.
12. Define each term in a full sentence when it first matters, and say what is outside the scope.
13. End each chapter with what it showed and what that provides for the next one.
14. Keep the final chapter short, and close on what the work is for.

**Short (adviser).** Formal, and impersonal in the parts that frame the work. Sentences are the longest of the three and often carry a result with its condition and its interpretation. Each contribution moves through the same verbs, which are formalized, developed, instantiated, and validated. A chapter opens with a short abstract and ends with a Summary. Claims are graded with words such as some support, limited evidence, and preliminary. The dissertation closes on an ethical concern of the field. Most frequent sentence openers are Additionally (24), However (21), Finally (20), For example (5).

**Vázquez.** Direct and orderly, with we throughout. Sentences are the shortest of the three, and colons and semicolons are the rarest. General statements are followed by For example, and reasons and limits are counted with First, Second, and Third. Discussion paragraphs open with a lead sentence that states the point. The goal of the dissertation is set apart in one sentence, and the scope is stated early. Most frequent sentence openers are For example (38), However (21), In general (20), In particular (16).

**Bobu.** Argumentative and vivid, with we throughout and I in the final chapter. One household example runs through most chapters. The approach is set against the usual one with rather than, instead, and by contrast. Evaluative words are stronger, and the register is at times conversational. Limits are stated at length, and each chapter says where it was published and with whom. Most frequent sentence openers are However (31), Unfortunately (15), Lastly (15), For example (12).

**Hang's draft.** In mean sentence length the draft is closest to Vázquez, in colons to Vázquez, and in semicolons to Vázquez and Bobu. It uses first person less than any of the three. Its structure, with an overview, contributions, an outline, and a Summary at the end of each chapter, is closest to Short's. Most frequent sentence openers are However (25), For instance (13), Moreover (11), Thus (10).

## Where the three agree

- A chapter is the subject of presents, introduces, describes, and reviews. See [Verbs for what a chapter does](#verbs-for-what-a-chapter-does).
- Terms are defined in a full sentence, and the scope is stated. See [Definitions in a full sentence](#definitions-in-a-full-sentence) and [Scope statements](#scope-statements).
- Hypotheses are numbered, and the verdict is attached to the result that decides it. See [Hypotheses and verdicts](#hypotheses-and-verdicts).
- A result is stated firmly, and its interpretation is hedged. A null result is reported as not found. See [A result with the clause that interprets it](#a-result-with-the-clause-that-interprets-it) and [A difference that was not found](#a-difference-that-was-not-found).
- An unexpected result gets possible explanations. See [Possible explanations](#possible-explanations).
- Limitations are plain and tied to the design. See [Limitations stated plainly](#limitations-stated-plainly).
- Future work comes from the scope and limits of the dissertation. See [Future work from scope and limits](#future-work-from-scope-and-limits).
- Each chapter says where it was published and who contributed. See [Where the work was published and who did it](#where-the-work-was-published-and-who-did-it).
- The introduction has one figure of the whole approach. See [Pointing to figures, sections, and chapters](#pointing-to-figures-sections-and-chapters).
- The final chapter is three to four pages. See [The final chapter](#the-final-chapter).

## Vocabulary

### Names for the document

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

Each writer settles on a small set of names for the document and repeats them. Short writes this dissertation (31 times in the chapters) and this work (55), and also this dissertation work for what was done during the degree. Vázquez writes this dissertation (21) and this work (26). Bobu uses this dissertation and this thesis as equivalents (5 and 7). None of them calls the document a paper.

**Reusable forms**

- This dissertation presents [contribution].
- In this work, [term] is defined as [definition].
- The remainder of this document is organized as follows.
- [Analysis or dataset] was [performed / collected] as part of this dissertation work.

**In Hang's dissertation.** Keep this dissertation for the whole program and this chapter for one study, as Hang's reference already says. This work is common in all three and is acceptable for a short reference to the program. Choose dissertation or thesis and use only that one. A chapter that came from a paper should not call itself this paper.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [Impersonal dissertation voice](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#impersonal-dissertation-voice) and [Established dissertation terminology](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#established-dissertation-terminology).

**Examples**

> This dissertation presents a domain-independent computational model of moderation of multi-party human-machine interactions that enables a robot or virtual agent to act as a moderator in a group interaction.
>
> — [Short, p. 1 (PDF page 19)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=19)

> This dissertation suggests to embrace dynamic group conversations in HRI.
>
> — [Vázquez, p. 2 (PDF page 9)](../dessertation_reference/Vazquez_2017_CMU_thesis.pdf#page=9)

> Overall, this thesis approaches interaction by explicitly tackling representation alignment, rather than hoping it will naturally emerge.
>
> — [Bobu, p. 2 (PDF page 16)](../dessertation_reference/Bobu_2023_Berkeley_EECS-2023-200.pdf#page=16)

### Verbs for what a chapter does

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

A chapter or the dissertation is the subject of a small set of verbs. Short uses presents, introduces, describes, reviews, and discusses. Vázquez uses presents, describes, and reviews. Bobu, who writes with we, uses propose, present, introduce, formalize, and showcase. The verb says what kind of content follows, so reviews announces literature, introduces announces a new model or method, and presents announces a study or its results.

**Reusable forms**

- This chapter reviews [literature] in [fields].
- This chapter introduces [model or method], in which [one-clause description].
- This chapter presents [number] studies that [purpose].
- This chapter describes [system or procedure].

**In Hang's dissertation.** These verbs match the ones in Hang's reference. A chapter can present, introduce, review, or describe. It does not collect data or find results. Formalize promises a formal definition, so use it only where one follows.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [Research actions](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#research-actions) and [Impersonal dissertation voice](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#impersonal-dissertation-voice).

**Examples**

> This chapter reviews relevant literature in socially assistive robotics, computer supported collaborative work, and human-robot interaction.
>
> — [Short, p. 10 (PDF page 28)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=28)

> This chapter reviews relevant background from social psychology.
>
> — [Vázquez, p. 6 (PDF page 13)](../dessertation_reference/Vazquez_2017_CMU_thesis.pdf#page=13)

> In this chapter, we formalize how autonomous systems can explicitly reason about how well they can explain given human inputs.
>
> — [Bobu, p. 117 (PDF page 131)](../dessertation_reference/Bobu_2023_Berkeley_EECS-2023-200.pdf#page=131)

### Develop, instantiate, validate, evaluate

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

Short moves each contribution through a fixed chain of verbs. A model is formalized, an algorithm is developed from it, the algorithm is instantiated for one task, and the instance is validated or evaluated in a study. The same verbs return in every chapter opening and summary, so the reader always knows which step a sentence reports. Vázquez uses designed and built for the platform, evaluated for a method on a benchmark, and validated for an idea that an experiment tests. Bobu uses test for hypotheses and showcase for a demonstration of a framework in one setting.

**Reusable forms**

- [Method] is instantiated for [task type] and evaluated in [study].
- [Idea] was validated in [setting] with [participants].
- [Framework] was evaluated on [benchmark or task] against [baselines].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Useful for the method chapters, where a general idea is followed by one implementation and one evaluation. Keep Hang's limit on validate, which needs a stated check. Evaluate does not say that the outcome was favorable.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [Research actions](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#research-actions) and [Method operations and their objects](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#method-operations-and-their-objects).

**Examples**

> This algorithm is instantiated for collaborative tasks with individual goals, where the moderator either equalizes or reinforces performance on the task.
>
> — [Short, p. 117 (PDF page 135)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=135)

> We evaluate this framework in a public computer vision benchmark for group detection, and show how it can be used to estimate the members of a robot’s group conversation in real-time.
>
> — [Vázquez, abstract (PDF page 2)](../dessertation_reference/Vazquez_2017_CMU_thesis.pdf#page=2)

> we showcase the framework for learning from demonstrations using user demonstration data for an arm motion planning task;
>
> — [Bobu, p. 118 (PDF page 132)](../dessertation_reference/Bobu_2023_Berkeley_EECS-2023-200.pdf#page=132)

[All 4 source examples for this pattern](source-evidence.md#develop-instantiate-validate-evaluate).

### Enable and leverage

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

Enable is the shared verb for what the research makes possible for a robot or a person. It appears 1.2, 0.9, and 1.4 times per 1,000 words in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Its object is an agent and an action, as in enable robots to detect. Leverage is rarer (0.2, 0.4, and 0.4 per 1,000 words) and means using something that already exists, such as a prior result, a dependency between two problems, or a dataset.

**Reusable forms**

- [Method] enables [agent] to [action] without [requirement].
- This chapter leverages [existing result or structure] to [purpose].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Both verbs are familiar to these readers, and leveraging is already in the title of the dissertation. Give enable an agent and an action. Hang's reference prefers the operation to a vague benefit, so follow enable with what the method does.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [Method operations and their objects](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#method-operations-and-their-objects) and [Practical purpose](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#practical-purpose).

**Examples**

> The model of moderation provides a framework for developing algorithms that enable robots to moderate group interactions without the need for speech recognition
>
> — [Short, abstract (PDF page 18)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=18)

> This chapter describes our efforts towards enabling robots to detect free-standing group conversations and their members.
>
> — [Vázquez, p. 39 (PDF page 46)](../dessertation_reference/Vazquez_2017_CMU_thesis.pdf#page=46)

> In this third part, we enable the robot to automatically detect when its representation is misaligned with the human’s and fix it.
>
> — [Bobu, p. 4 (PDF page 18)](../dessertation_reference/Bobu_2023_Berkeley_EECS-2023-200.pdf#page=18)

### Insight, intuition, and observation

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

The central idea of a method is named as an idea before it is implemented. Vázquez writes our main insight is that and our key methodological insight. Bobu writes the core insight of this dissertation, the intuition is that, and our observation is that. Short uses insight for what a study yields, as in provides key insight into. The sentence that follows the label is short enough to remember and is repeated in the abstract, the chapter, and the conclusion.

**Reusable forms**

- The main insight of this chapter is that [relationship].
- The intuition is that if [condition], then [consequence].
- [Study] provides insight into [question].

**In Hang's dissertation.** This is the handoff from a finding to a method in Hang's reference. State the intuition once in a sentence and reuse that sentence. In the impersonal voice, the insight belongs to the chapter or the method.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [An intuition leading to an operation](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#an-intuition-leading-to-an-operation) and [A study finding leading to a method](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#a-study-finding-leading-to-a-method).

**Examples**

> The nutrition study provides key insight into how child-robot interactions develop over time, as well as providing insight for how a robot can use games to support cognitive skill development.
>
> — [Short, p. 6 (PDF page 24)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=24)

> Our main insight is that we can exploit the mutual dependency between two problems for this task.
>
> — [Vázquez, p. 2 (PDF page 9)](../dessertation_reference/Vazquez_2017_CMU_thesis.pdf#page=9)

> The intuition is that if a person decides two robot behaviors are similar, they must also internally represent them similarly, enabling us to extract their representation.
>
> — [Bobu, p. 4 (PDF page 18)](../dessertation_reference/Bobu_2023_Berkeley_EECS-2023-200.pdf#page=18)

### People in the studies

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

Participants is the word inside a study for all three. Outside a study the words differ by field. Short writes users, target users, and group members. Vázquez writes people, users, and interactants. Bobu writes the human or the person for the teacher in a method, end-users for the people a system is built for, and non-expert users or novice users when experience matters. Each study states the number of participants, how they were recruited, and the limits of the sample.

**Reusable forms**

- [N] participants were recruited from [population].
- Participants experienced one of [N] conditions.
- Non-expert users can use [approach] to [teach / provide] [signal].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Hang's reference uses non-experts for the population, participants inside a study, demonstrators, and annotators. Bobu's non-expert users and novice users show that the term is familiar to this committee. Keep Hang's four terms and do not add novice as a synonym.

The related pattern in Hang's reference is [Teachers and collection settings](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#teachers-and-collection-settings).

**Examples**

> A key component of the development of any SAR system is understanding the target users population that the robot will be assisting.
>
> — [Short, p. 41 (PDF page 59)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=59)

> Twenty groups of 3 or 4 children interacted with Chester and Blink, for a total of 74 participants.
>
> — [Vázquez, p. 20 (PDF page 27)](../dessertation_reference/Vazquez_2017_CMU_thesis.pdf#page=27)

> In a user study with the JACO2 (Kinova) robotic arm, we show that non-expert users can use our approach for learning features (Sec. 3.5.2).

> — [Bobu, p. 22 (PDF page 36)](../dessertation_reference/Bobu_2023_Berkeley_EECS-2023-200.pdf#page=36)

### Words for imperfect human input

*Seen in Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

Bobu's dissertation is the closest of the three to Hang's topic. Input from non-experts is called noisy, and features learned from it are called noisy or imperfect. The loss relative to experts is called degradation in input quality or degradation in performance. The claim that follows is measured against two reference points, an expert and a random baseline. Noisy input is described as input that still carries signal.

**Reusable forms**

- [Non-expert input] was closer to [expert level] than to [random baseline] on [measure].
- When given imperfect [inputs], [method] still [outcome] relative to [baseline].
- With very noisy [inputs], the results are less conclusive.

**In Hang's dissertation.** Hang's reference uses imperfections as the umbrella term and asks that noisy, sub-optimal, and unsuccessful each keep an operational definition. Bobu's wording supports the same stance, which is that imperfect teaching still contains information. The two reference points, expert and random, are a form worth reusing where the data allow it.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [Imperfect teaching and structured behavior](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#imperfect-teaching-and-structured-behavior) and [Describing human teaching without blame](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#describing-human-teaching-without-blame).

**Examples**

> While this does not mean that user features are as good as expert features (we expect some degradation in performance when going to non-experts), it shows that […] the user features maintain a lot of signal despite this degradation.
>
> — [Bobu, p. 41 (PDF page 55)](../dessertation_reference/Bobu_2023_Berkeley_EECS-2023-200.pdf#page=55)

[All 4 source examples for this pattern](source-evidence.md#words-for-imperfect-human-input).

### Graded words for evidence

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

The strength of a finding is graded in words that sit next to the number. Short writes some support, limited evidence, modest evidence, preliminary evidence, marginal significance, and a trend towards. Vázquez writes some evidence of and a trend suggested. Bobu writes provide evidence that, less conclusive, and less clear results. Strong words such as demonstrate and show are kept for results with a significant test or a clear margin.

**Reusable forms**

- [Analysis] provides preliminary evidence for [pattern].
- There was limited evidence that [outcome] (H[n]).
- [Result] showed a trend towards [direction] ([statistic]).
- The results are less conclusive when [condition].

**In Hang's dissertation.** This agrees with Hang's rule that claim strength follows the evidence. Put the value next to the graded word, as Hang's reference asks for correlations. Marginal significance and trend need the p value beside them.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [Hedges and degree words](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#hedges-and-degree-words) and [Reporting findings and interpretations](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#reporting-findings-and-interpretations).

**Examples**

> In terms of the educational goal of the intervention, we find limited evidence that the children in the study learned about nutrition over the intervention
>

### Suggest, may, might, could

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

Interpretations are hedged with a small set of words, and each writer has favorites. Suggest and its forms appear 1.2, 1.3, and 0.7 times per 1,000 words in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. May appears 0.8, 0.9, and 1.9 times, might 1.0, 0.2, and 1.0 times, and could 0.7, 1.9, and 1.2 times. Short prefers might and suggesting that. Vázquez prefers could and tend to. Bobu prefers may. Hang's draft has 1.2 for suggest, 1.1 for may, 0.3 for might, and 1.2 for could, which is inside the range of the three.

**Reusable forms**

- [Result], suggesting that [bounded interpretation].
- This finding might indicate [interpretation].
- [Group] tended to [behavior] when [condition].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Hang's reference gives each modal its own job, and the three dissertations use them the same way. No change in the amount of hedging is needed to match these readers. Keep the rule from the Chapter 8 review that can is not lowered to may as a matter of routine.

The related pattern in Hang's reference is [Hedges and degree words](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#hedges-and-degree-words).

### First person and the impersonal voice

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Differs from Hang's preference, which takes priority.*

Vázquez and Bobu use we throughout, also for work by the single author. Bobu changes to I in the final chapter for her own plans. Vázquez writes the author where she separates her part from a team's.

**Reusable forms**

- [Algorithm] is validated in [study], and it is shown that [result].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Hang's dissertation is impersonal by default and that decision stands. Short's dissertation shows the same voice in the framing parts, so the adviser's own writing supports it. ### A result with its test

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

A quantitative result is one sentence that names the measure, gives the two values with their spread in parentheses, and gives the test with its statistic and p value. Short gives M and SD, the test, and often an effect size. Vázquez gives the test in parentheses after the factor name and M and SE for each condition. Bobu names the test and its factors in words and gives F and p. The verb is plain, such as showed, revealed, was significant, or found.

**Reusable forms**

- Participants had higher [measure] in [condition A] (M = [a], SD = [b]) than in [condition B] (M = [c], SD = [d]).
- A [test] on [measure] showed [result] (p = [value]).
- [Factor] was significant ([statistic], p = [value]).

**In Hang's dissertation.** Same content as Hang's reference, which asks for the measure, baseline, condition, and uncertainty together. Hang's reference uses compared with in a sentence and against inside parentheses. Do not add a test or a spread that the chapter did not compute.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [Numbers and measured comparisons](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#numbers-and-measured-comparisons) and [A results paragraph answering one comparison](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#a-results-paragraph-answering-one-comparison).

**Examples**

> Gaze was significant (F[1, 68] = 5.67, p = 0.02): participants stood significantly farther away from the robot with RG (M = 2.29, SE=0.05) than with AG (M = 2.09, SE = 0.06).
>

> We ran an ANOVA with input type as a factor and task as a covariate, […] finding a significant main effect (F(2, 103) = 132.7505, p < .0001).
>
> — [Bobu, p. 41 (PDF page 55)](../dessertation_reference/Bobu_2023_Berkeley_EECS-2023-200.pdf#page=55)

### A difference that was not found

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

A null result is reported as something the analysis did not find, and the sentence does not claim that the conditions are equal. Short writes we did not find significant differences and this trend did not reach statistical significance, and often adds a likely reason. Vázquez writes was not significant and there were no significant differences. Bobu reports no significant difference and then says what the result does not mean.

**Reusable forms**

- No significant difference was found in [measure] between [conditions].
- [Trend with values], however this trend did not reach statistical significance.
- This does not mean that [A] is as good as [B]. It shows that [bounded statement].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Hang's preferred wording is did not detect a difference, with the warning that this does not mean the two conditions are equivalent. The three use find where Hang uses detect, and the stance is the same. Bobu's second sentence is the same warning written out.

**Examples**

### Hypotheses and verdicts

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

Hypotheses are numbered and stated before the results. The verdict is attached to the result that decides it, with supporting H1, in line with H2, contrary to H4, or a label in parentheses such as H6 and H7 unsupported. Partial support is said in words. Short separates the part of a hypothesis that held from the part that did not. Bobu adds a caution to a supported hypothesis when the evidence for one part is weaker.

**Reusable forms**

- [Result with statistic], supporting H[n].
- Thus the first part of H[n], [content], is supported, but not [the second part].
- These results support H[n], although the evidence for [part] is weaker.
- No support was found for the hypothesis that [content].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Same practice as Hang's reference. Keep the verdict in the sentence that reports the deciding result, which matches Hang's preference for keeping a result and its interpretation together.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [Hypotheses and verdicts](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#hypotheses-and-verdicts) and [A short verdict after the evidence](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#a-short-verdict-after-the-evidence).

**Examples**

### Possible explanations

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

An unexpected or weak result is followed by one or two candidate reasons, each marked as a possibility. Short writes this may have been because and may have been due to the fact that, and likely due to for the reason she finds most plausible. Vázquez writes could be attributed to the fact that, an alternative explanation is that, and part of the problem could have been. Bobu writes it is unclear whether and names the two readings that the data cannot separate.

**Reusable forms**

- This may have been because [reason A], or may have been due to [reason B].
- [Result] could be attributed to [reason]. An alternative explanation is that [reason].
- It is unclear whether [outcome] is due to [A] or to [B].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Same as Hang's reference, which presents reasons as hypotheses that could be examined. Bobu's it is unclear whether is a useful form when two explanations remain open.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [Possible explanations](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#possible-explanations) and [An unexpected result and possible reasons](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#an-unexpected-result-and-possible-reasons).

**Examples**

> — [Short, p. 114 (PDF page 132)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=132)

> The bigger separation between the robot and the participants that we observed in the experiment could be attributed to the fact that Chester is different – it is a robot, not a person.

### Limitations stated plainly

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

A limitation is announced in a short sentence and then listed with its consequence. Vázquez opens a discussion with the experiment was limited in several ways and counts the limits with First, Second, and Third. **Reusable forms**

- The [study] was limited in several ways. First, [limit]. Second, [limit].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Same as Hang's reference, where a limitation names the design constraint and the claim it limits. Bobu's caution about a technical sample is a model for the studies with recruited non-experts and crowdworkers.

**Examples**

> This study, like most, features a small number of participants and a limited amount of total interaction time with those participants (Scassellati et al., 2012).

> — [Short, p. 79 (PDF page 97)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=97)

### Wording for future work

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

**Reusable forms**

- Further research is needed to determine whether [question].
- Future work could [action], which would [benefit].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Hang's reference uses Future work could and One planned step is to, with would for what a planned study does. ### Scope statements

**Reusable forms**

**In Hang's dissertation.** Hang's reference has no separate pattern for scope, so this one adds to it. **Reusable forms**

## Writing style

### A concrete scene before the abstraction

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

A problem is shown in a small scene before it is named in general terms. Vázquez asks the reader to consider a figure in which two people talk near a robot. Bobu uses a robot that hands a user a cup of coffee. Short gives two users who respond differently to the same request. The scene takes one to three sentences and is followed by the general statement it illustrates.

**Reusable forms**

- For example, consider [figure], in which [situation].
- To [goal], the robot [considers / needs] [quantities]. [What goes wrong without them].
- One user might [response A], while another might [response B].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Same as the hypothetical scenario in Hang's reference. Label a hypothetical case as one, keep it short, and return to the technical point.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [A hypothetical scenario that explains a problem](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#a-hypothetical-scenario-that-explains-a-problem) and [An example in a few connected sentences](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#an-example-in-a-few-connected-sentences).

**Examples**

> — [Short, p. 170 (PDF page 188)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=188)

> To act in the world, robots rely on a representation of salient features of the task: for instance, to hand a user a cup of coffee, the robot may consider movement efficiency and cup orientation in its behavior.
>
> — [Bobu, p. 1 (PDF page 15)](../dessertation_reference/Bobu_2023_Berkeley_EECS-2023-200.pdf#page=15)

### One example carried through

*Seen in Vázquez and Bobu. Adds to Hang's reference.*

Bobu returns to one household scene, a robot that carries a cup near a laptop, in the introduction and in most chapters. Each new idea is explained by changing one detail of that scene, with phrases such as in our earlier example and in the above example. Vázquez does the same with her robot and its named behaviors. The reader learns the example once and afterwards attends only to what changed.

**Reusable forms**

- In the earlier example, if [changed condition], [consequence].
- In the scenario in [figure], [actor] wants [goal].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Hang's chapters use different tasks, so one example for the whole dissertation is not available. Inside a chapter, the task of that chapter can serve as the running example for the signal, the method, and the result.

### The gap, then the response

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

The motivation names what existing work covers, what it leaves out, and why. Vázquez writes that a problem has often been overlooked, lists the related problems that have been studied, and gives a reason for the gap. Bobu states what typical methods do, the limit of that approach, and the alternative introduced by Instead. Short states what prior work has primarily focused on and the case in which that focus does not hold. The gap is specific, and prior work is described without dismissal.

**Reusable forms**

- [Problem] has often been overlooked by [community]. However, these methods do not address [specific problem]. One reason for this gap is [reason].
- Typical methods [approach], but [limit]. Instead, [alternative].
- Prior work in [area] has primarily focused on [scope]. There are exceptions, however, when [condition].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Same as the introduction pattern in Hang's reference, which is built around a particular assumption about human teaching. Vázquez's third sentence, the reason for the gap, is a useful addition.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [An introduction built around a consequential assumption](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#an-introduction-built-around-a-consequential-assumption) and [Prior work and research gaps](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#prior-work-and-research-gaps).

**Examples**

> There are exceptions to this, however, when fostering social interactions between people is a goal of the system.
>
> — [Short, p. 6 (PDF page 24)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=24)

> The problem of detecting social conversations and their members has often been overlooked by the HRI community.
>
> — [Vázquez, p. 1 (PDF page 8)](../dessertation_reference/Vazquez_2017_CMU_thesis.pdf#page=8)

> Typical methods attempt to learn the human’s representation of what matters in a task from aggregated data sets of human behavior, but this data will also not reflect every individual, environment, and task the robot will be exposed to.
>
> — [Bobu, p. 2 (PDF page 16)](../dessertation_reference/Bobu_2023_Berkeley_EECS-2023-200.pdf#page=16)

### The thesis in one sentence

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Adds to Hang's reference.*

Each dissertation can be reduced to one sentence, and the writer provides it. Vázquez sets the goal of the dissertation apart from the paragraph. Bobu sets the core insight apart in the same way. Short opens the abstract with the sentence and repeats it word for word in the introduction. The sentence names the object, such as a model or computational models, and what it makes possible.

**Reusable forms**

- The goal of this dissertation is to [verb] [object] for [purpose].
- This dissertation presents [object] that enables [agent] to [action].

**In Hang's dissertation.** This adds to Hang's reference. Check that the Abstract, the Overview, and the Conclusion state the thesis in the same words, and that the three stages follow from it.

The related pattern in Hang's reference is [The three stage dissertation arc](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#the-three-stage-dissertation-arc).

**Examples**

> This dissertation presents a domain-independent computational model of moderation of multi-party human-machine interactions that enables a robot or virtual agent to act as a moderator in a group interaction.
>
> — [Short, abstract (PDF page 17)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=17)

> the goal of this dissertation is to develop computational models for robots to detect and sustain the spatial patterns of human behavior that naturally emerge during free-standing group conversations.
>
> — [Vázquez, p. 2 (PDF page 9)](../dessertation_reference/Vazquez_2017_CMU_thesis.pdf#page=9)

> successful human-robot interaction requires robots to engage with humans in an interactive process for finding a shared representation of what’s salient for the task.

### Research as iteration

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

The dissertation is presented as a cycle of studying people, building a capability, and evaluating it. Short calls this an iterative approach and Vázquez an iterative research methodology, and both show it in a figure. Each study is said to inform, motivate, or enable the next step. A limitation observed in one study is named as the reason for the following chapter.

**Reusable forms**

- The results of this study motivate [next step].
- This limitation motivated [the work in the next chapter].
- These studies inform the development of [method or behavior].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Hang's three stages, understand, mitigate, and leverage, are the same kind of story. Hang's reference adds a boundary, which is that a study can motivate a method without validating it, and that the stages are a conceptual dependency.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [The three stage dissertation arc](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#the-three-stage-dissertation-arc) and [A study finding leading to a method](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#a-study-finding-leading-to-a-method).

**Examples**

> This work presents an iterative approach to developing the model of moderation, that includes studies and analyses to develop an understanding of the application domains for robot moderators that inform the development of algorithms based on our model of moderation.
>
> — [Short, p. 2 (PDF page 20)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=20)

> This methodology also drives the organization of the rest of this document.
>
> — [Vázquez, p. 4 (PDF page 11)](../dessertation_reference/Vazquez_2017_CMU_thesis.pdf#page=11)

> In the following sections, we explore whether both expert and non-expert features can be used to improve reward learning generalization.
>
> — [Bobu, p. 42 (PDF page 56)](../dessertation_reference/Bobu_2023_Berkeley_EECS-2023-200.pdf#page=56)

### Implications for other researchers

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

A discussion says what the result means for people who build or study similar systems. Short addresses designers and researchers directly and says under which goal a design choice is preferable. Vázquez states that the work has design implications and that a protocol could be used to study other questions. Bobu titles paragraphs Implications for a named setting. The implication is conditional on a goal or a setting.

**Reusable forms**

- For [designers / researchers] of [systems], these results [suggest / provide] [implication].
- For scenarios where [goal] is most important, [design choice] may be preferable.
- [Protocol or method] could be used to study [other question].

**In Hang's dissertation.** This matches the practical purpose and the methodological implication in Hang's reference. Keep could and may for uses that were not tested.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [Practical purpose](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#practical-purpose) and [Discussion synthesis and its boundary](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#discussion-synthesis-and-its-boundary).

**Examples**

### Observations and participants' words

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

Numbers are accompanied by what was seen and what participants said. Short reports observations with we observed and quotes single words and remarks. Vázquez quotes children's utterances and describes one session frame by frame in a figure. Bobu quotes written remarks from the survey in parentheses after the result they explain. The quotation is short, is attributed to one participant or several, and supports a point that the numbers already make.

**Reusable forms**

- Participants [general observation], and in the words of one participant, [quoted remark].
- Several participants mentioned that [content] ([quoted remark]).
- For example, one participant [action or remark], which illustrates [pattern].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Same as the observed episode in Hang's reference. Use only episodes and remarks that the study recorded, and do not treat one remark as evidence of how common something is.

### Candor about costs

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Adds to Hang's reference.*

All three say plainly when something did not work or came at a cost. Short reports that the robot's assistance did come at a cost and gives the lower task performance. Vázquez writes Unfortunately when a difference was too small to interpret, and of course, we pay a price for a method's flexibility. Bobu writes that a method is not the full answer and lists what it does not solve. The admission is specific and is followed by what can still be concluded.

**Reusable forms**

- However, [benefit] did come at a cost, since [measured cost].
- This difference was small enough that it had no functional meaning.
- [Method] does not solve [problem] fully. [What it improves].

**In Hang's dissertation.** This adds to Hang's reference. It fits the dissertation's own argument, since imperfect teaching is its subject. Bobu's idioms are replaced by literal wording under Hang's rule.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [Literal wording](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#literal-wording) and [An unexpected result and possible reasons](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#an-unexpected-result-and-possible-reasons).

**Examples**

> However, the robot’s assistance did come at a cost: the performance of the group was higher in the unmoderated testing sessions (H6 and H7 unsupported)
>
> — [Short, p. 134 (PDF page 152)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=152)

> Unfortunately, this difference was small enough, that we thought it had no functional meaning.
>
> — [Vázquez, p. 37 (PDF page 44)](../dessertation_reference/Vazquez_2017_CMU_thesis.pdf#page=44)

> That said, we need to be explicit that this is not the be-all end-all solution to our goal above.
>
> — [Bobu, p. 111 (PDF page 125)](../dessertation_reference/Bobu_2023_Berkeley_EECS-2023-200.pdf#page=125)

### A step, not an end point

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

The size of the contribution is stated modestly. Vázquez calls the dissertation our first steps toward a research agenda. Bobu calls a chapter merely a step towards understanding and a step towards recovering what she wants to recover. Short says that results provide a basis for further algorithms and that evidence is preliminary. The modest frame is paired with a clear statement of what was shown.

**Reusable forms**

- [Work] is a step towards [larger goal].
- These results provide a basis for [next development].

**In Hang's dissertation.** This agrees with Hang's restraint on novelty claims. One such sentence in a chapter is enough, and it should not replace the statement of the finding.

The related pattern in Hang's reference is [Value words and novelty](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#value-words-and-novelty).

**Examples**

> These results support the use of a robot as the moderator in a group interaction, and provide a basis for the development of further moderation algorithms.
>
> — [Short, p. 116 (PDF page 134)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=134)

> This dissertation presented our first steps toward a research agenda where we aim to make robot perception cognizant of social behavior for HRI.
>
> — [Vázquez, p. 81 (PDF page 88)](../dessertation_reference/Vazquez_2017_CMU_thesis.pdf#page=88)

> Our work is merely a step towards understanding how explicitly focusing on learning features can impact reward learning generalization and sample complexity.
>
> — [Bobu, p. 54 (PDF page 68)](../dessertation_reference/Bobu_2023_Berkeley_EECS-2023-200.pdf#page=68)

### Register

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Differs from Hang's preference, which takes priority.*

Short and Vázquez write in a formal register throughout. Bobu mixes a formal register with conversational touches, such as contractions, idioms, and questions to the reader. Contractions appear 0.3, 0.2, and 0.9 times per 1,000 words in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. All of Short's are inside the quoted speech of participants or of the robot, and Vázquez has a few of her own. Bobu's idioms include from the get-go and the be-all end-all solution.

**Reusable forms**

- [Subject] is modeled as [formal description], in which [components].
- This dependency implies that [consequence].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Hang's reference asks for literal wording, and Hang's draft has no contractions. That is the register of Short and Vázquez. Bobu's conversational touches are her own voice and are not a model for this dissertation.

The related pattern in Hang's reference is [Literal wording](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#literal-wording).

**Examples**

> In this work, moderation is modeled as a cyclic decision-making process, in which the moderator monitors the interaction state, evaluates the state relative to some number of task and social goals, and chooses social behaviors that change the interaction.
>
> — [Short, p. 101 (PDF page 119)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=119)

> This dependency implies that robots should reason about and control their gaze and body motion jointly.
>
> — [Vázquez, p. 67 (PDF page 74)](../dessertation_reference/Vazquez_2017_CMU_thesis.pdf#page=74)

> So far, we assumed that the human can identify every individual feature of the representation they wish to teach, but that’s not always easy.

### Ending on the wider stake

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Adds to Hang's reference.*

The last paragraph of each dissertation leaves the technical content and states what the work is for. Short ends with an ethical concern of her field and how her approach answers it. Vázquez ends a future direction with what it could teach about human conversation. Bobu ends by asking a broader research question and describing the systems that would benefit. The closing is short and is not a summary.

**Reusable forms**

- One consistent concern in [field] is that [concern]. This dissertation demonstrates another path, which is [approach].
- Ultimately, these efforts could help [wider benefit].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Hang's Conclusion closes with the hope that future work will embrace the imperfections in non-expert teaching, which is this move. Short's closing names a concern of the field and says how her approach answers it. The content of a closing has to come from Hang. A closing for the whole dissertation does not conflict with Hang's rule against adding a closing sentence to each future-work paragraph.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [Future work drafted from notes](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#future-work-drafted-from-notes) and [Practical purpose](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#practical-purpose).

**Examples**

## Sentence style

### Sentence length

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

The three write sentences of different lengths, and Hang's draft is within their range. The mean is 26.1 words in Short, 20.3 in Vázquez, and 22.8 in Bobu, against 20.7 in Hang's draft. Sentences of 35 words or more make up 22 percent of Short's, 6 percent of Vázquez's, and 13 percent of Bobu's, against 10 percent of the draft's. Short's long sentences carry a result with its conditions and its interpretation. Vázquez breaks the same content into two or three sentences. All three place a short sentence where a point has to stand out.

**Reusable forms**

- [Short statement of the point]. [Longer sentence with the evidence and its condition].
- One possible direction for future work is [topic].

**In Hang's dissertation.** No change in sentence length is needed to suit these readers. Hang's rule of one main job for each sentence decides where to split, and a result stays with the which clause that interprets it.

The related pattern in Hang's reference is [One main job and varied sentence length](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#one-main-job-and-varied-sentence-length).

**Examples**

> One possible direction for future work is personalized moderation.
>
> — [Short, p. 170 (PDF page 188)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=188)

> This analysis was inconclusive and there were no significant differences.
>
> — [Vázquez, p. 27 (PDF page 34)](../dessertation_reference/Vazquez_2017_CMU_thesis.pdf#page=34)

> Human behavior is not random – it is intentional and reflective of internal goals and preferences.
>
> — [Bobu, p. 4 (PDF page 18)](../dessertation_reference/Bobu_2023_Berkeley_EECS-2023-200.pdf#page=18)

### A short sentence that states the point

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

A paragraph often begins with a short declarative sentence that says what the paragraph establishes. Vázquez sets this sentence in small capitals at the start of most discussion paragraphs, so that a discussion can be read from these sentences alone. Bobu and Short use the same kind of sentence without the typography. The sentence is a claim or a finding and has a subject and a verb.

**Reusable forms**

- [Factor] had effects on [outcome].
- There were no differences in [measure] between conditions.
- The quality of [output] depends directly on the quality of [input].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Hang's reference asks for discussion headings with a concrete subject. Vázquez's lead sentences are a second way to do this without adding headings. A lead sentence must not claim more than the paragraph shows.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [Headings that name the question or finding](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#headings-that-name-the-question-or-finding) and [Direct subjects and concrete details](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#direct-subjects-and-concrete-details).

**Examples**

> The quality of the learned rewards depends directly on the quality of the learned features.
>
> — [Bobu, p. 54 (PDF page 68)](../dessertation_reference/Bobu_2023_Berkeley_EECS-2023-200.pdf#page=68)

### Openers that mark the step

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

Sentences often open with a word that tells the reader how the sentence relates to the one before. However opens 21 sentences in Short, 21 in Vázquez, and 31 in Bobu. Short's other frequent openers are Additionally (24) and Finally (20). Vázquez's are For example (38), In general (20), In particular (16), and Moreover (15). Bobu's are Unfortunately (15), Lastly (15), and For example (12). The last item of a series is marked with Finally or Lastly.

**Reusable forms**

- However, [contrast].
- Additionally, [further result of the same kind].
- In particular, [the specific case].
- Finally, [last item of the series].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Same practice as Hang's reference, where However opens its sentence and each connector has one job. Hang's reference accepts Additionally and In addition for an added point. Unfortunately is Bobu's habit and is a value word.

The related pattern in Hang's reference is [Connectors with a clear job](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#connectors-with-a-clear-job).

### Colons and semicolons

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Differs from Hang's preference, which takes priority.*

All three use more of these marks than Hang now wants, and Hang's draft is already near the low end of their range. Colons appear 5.3, 3.1, and 6.1 times per 1,000 words in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu, and 3.6 times in the draft. Semicolons appear 2.3, 0.7, and 1.1 times, and 0.9 times in the draft. A colon introduces a list, an explanation of the clause before it, or an example. A semicolon joins two parallel statements, and Short uses it most. Vázquez is the most sparing with both.

**Reusable forms**

- [Lead-in that announces a list]: [items].
- One problem is [A]. The other one is [B].
- [Statement], since [explanation].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Hang's preference for few colons and semicolons takes priority, and nothing in the three dissertations argues against it. Readers who write with more of these marks will not find the draft heavy. Vázquez's sentence with one problem and the other one is the form that Hang's reference already uses, written there as two sentences.

### Parentheses for specifics

### Tense by section

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Adds to Hang's reference.*

Tense follows the kind of content. An overview of a chapter is in the present (this chapter presents). A procedure that was carried out is in the past (we conducted, were recruited). A model or algorithm is described in the present (the moderator monitors). A result is in the past, and its interpretation is in the present (this suggests). A summary of a chapter and the final chapter are in the past (this chapter presented, this dissertation presented). Short's study discussions state findings in the present with we find that.

**Reusable forms**

- This chapter presents [content].
- [Study] was conducted with [participants].
- [Method] infers [quantity] from [input].
- [Measure] was higher in [condition], which suggests that [interpretation].
- This chapter presented [content].

**In Hang's dissertation.** This adds to Hang's reference, which has no pattern for tense. The scheme above is the one that all three follow. It gives a rule for a passage whose tense is in doubt, which is past for what was done in a study and for a summary, and present for what a method does and for what a result suggests.

The related pattern in Hang's reference is [Reporting findings and interpretations](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#reporting-findings-and-interpretations).

**Examples**

> This chapter introduces the second moderation algorithm, in which the robot moderator takes actions based on the group members’ performance on a shared task.
>
> — [Short, p. 117 (PDF page 135)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=135)

> We conducted the experiment as a field trial during the 2013 Summer Games event at Disney Research Pittsburgh.
>
> — [Vázquez, p. 20 (PDF page 27)](../dessertation_reference/Vazquez_2017_CMU_thesis.pdf#page=27)

> We introduced feature traces as a novel type of human input that allows for intuitive teaching of non-linear features from high-dimensional state spaces.
>
> — [Bobu, p. 53 (PDF page 67)](../dessertation_reference/Bobu_2023_Berkeley_EECS-2023-200.pdf#page=67)

### A result with the clause that interprets it

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

A result and its interpretation are often one sentence. Vázquez and Bobu use it a few times, and Bobu also uses which means that. The interpretation is hedged, and the result is not.

**Reusable forms**

**In Hang's dissertation.** This is the structure that Hang kept in the Chapter 8 review, where a result stayed together with its which suggests that clause. Hang's form is which suggests that. ### Concession first

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

A sentence grants a point and then states the main one. The granted point opens the sentence with While, Although, or Even though, and the main clause follows. All three open with While. Short and Bobu also use Although, and Vázquez uses Even though. The pattern is common where a result is mixed, a method has a limit, or the scope is narrower than the general case.

**Reusable forms**

- While [what was not found], [what was found], suggesting that [interpretation].
- Although [general case], this dissertation focuses on [narrower case].
- While [method] enables [benefit], there are several limitations that [consequence].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Same as Hang's reference, which keeps the focus of the sentence in the second clause. Hang also places while after the main clause for an added contrast.

The related pattern in Hang's reference is [Concession with while](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#concession-with-while).

**Examples**

### Rather than and instead

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

**Reusable forms**

- [Approach] by [what it does], rather than [the usual alternative].
- [Behaviors A and B] should be [treated] jointly, rather than independently of each other.

**In Hang's dissertation.** Same job as the literal alternative in Hang's reference, which also offers the short form with not and whereas. The usual alternative has to be one that the cited work takes.

### First, Second, Third

### This with a noun

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

The next sentence picks up the previous one with This or These and a noun that names it, such as this approach, this framework, this limitation, this validation, this dependency, or these insights. The noun classifies what was just said, which tells the reader how to take it. Short and Vázquez rarely use This without a noun, and Bobu does so more often.

**Reusable forms**

- This [approach / framework] provides [what it provides].
- This limitation motivated [next step].
- This validation is important because [reason].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Hang's reference lets the next sentence take up what the previous one introduced, and accepts plain This when it refers to the statement just made. The noun is the safer choice when more than one referent is possible.

### Parallel sentences for conditions

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

**Reusable forms**

- In the former case, [description]. In the latter, [description].
- [Condition A] improved [X] but reduced [Y], and [condition B] improved [Y] but reduced [X].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Same as Hang's reference. Give each side of the comparison its own subject and verb, which is Hang's rule for complete clauses.

### The more, the more

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Adds to Hang's reference.*

A relationship between two quantities is stated as a proportion in words. Vázquez uses it for a general fact about sensing, Short for a measured relationship between how often the robot spoke to a participant and what the participant reported, and Bobu for the cost of a larger state space. The sentence gives the direction of the relationship, and the statistic follows when the relationship was measured.

**Reusable forms**

- The more [quantity A], the more [quantity B] ([statistic]).
- The higher [quantity A], the more [quantity B] is needed.

**In Hang's dissertation.** This adds to Hang's reference. Hang's rule is to give the correlation value, so a measured relationship written this way needs its r value in the same sentence. For a general fact, a citation takes that place.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [Numbers and measured comparisons](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#numbers-and-measured-comparisons) and [Matched factors and endpoints](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#matched-factors-and-endpoints).

**Examples**

### Because at the start

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

A reason that governs the whole sentence is placed first. All three open sentences with Because when a design decision follows from a property of the problem or from an earlier result. Short uses it to justify the evaluation domain, Vázquez to explain why a second protocol was designed, and Bobu to explain why a class of methods has its form. Bobu more often opens such a sentence with Since.

**Reusable forms**

- Because [property of the problem], [design decision].
- Because [earlier observation], [next study or method] was designed to [purpose].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Hang's reference uses since for a reason and keeps a governing condition attached to its claim. Because at the start of a sentence does the same job and is common in all three.

The related pattern in Hang's reference is [Local reasons and governing conditions](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#local-reasons-and-governing-conditions).

## Storytelling style

### The introduction

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

The three introductions open in different ways and cover the same ground. Vázquez starts from an application need, shows a scene, names the overlooked problem, states the goal, explains why the problem has structure, names the main insight, lists the challenges, limits the scope, and outlines the chapters. Bobu starts from failures of deployed systems, asks what is missing, builds one example, states her position and core insight, and walks through the parts. Short starts from a definition, states what the dissertation presents, explains the evaluation domain, describes the approach with a figure, and lists contributions and the outline. All three reach the statement of what the dissertation does within the first two pages.

**Reusable forms**

- Need → scene → gap → goal → insight → challenges → scope → outline.
- Failures → question → example → position → core insight → parts.
- Definition → what is presented → domain → approach figure → contributions → outline.

**In Hang's dissertation.** Hang's Introduction has an Overview, three sections on the studies, Contributions, and an Outline, which is closest to Short's. Hang's reference builds the opening around an assumption about human teaching, which is the gap move in Vázquez and Bobu.

The related pattern in Hang's reference is [An introduction built around a consequential assumption](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#an-introduction-built-around-a-consequential-assumption).

**Examples**

> In this work, moderation is defined as the process of controlling or directing a group interaction.
>
> — [Short, p. 1 (PDF page 19)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=19)

> The success of many robots in unstructured, human environments hinges on their ability to appropriately interact with and around people [42, 127].
>
> — [Vázquez, p. 1 (PDF page 8)](../dessertation_reference/Vazquez_2017_CMU_thesis.pdf#page=8)

> Despite this rise of autonomous system integration, seamless robot interaction with people remains elusive: autonomous cars still cause crashes in unconventional situations [202], flight control systems create tug of war scenarios in spite of conflicting pilot interventions [105], and, as we will see in this thesis, personal robots move objects uncomfortably close to their users [43].
>
> — [Bobu, p. 1 (PDF page 15)](../dessertation_reference/Bobu_2023_Berkeley_EECS-2023-200.pdf#page=15)

[All 4 source examples for this pattern](source-evidence.md#the-introduction).

### An outline with content

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

The outline says what each chapter contributes. Short gives one sentence for each chapter with a verb that fits its content. Vázquez gives a paragraph for each chapter that includes the question, the main result, and the venue. Bobu gives a paragraph for each part that explains why the part is needed and how the chapters in it differ. Inside a chapter, Bobu also lists what each section does.

**Reusable forms**

- [Chapter] reviews [literature] and describes [early work] that motivates [approach].
- ([Chapter]) [Title]. [Question]. [Approach]. [Main result].
- [Section] frames [problem]. [Section] presents [method]. [Section] presents the results of [study].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Hang's Outline has one sentence for each chapter, which is Short's form. Hang's reference asks for a roadmap with actual content. Vázquez's version puts the main result of each chapter in the outline, which lets a reader see the argument of the dissertation on one page.

The related pattern in Hang's reference is [A roadmap with actual content](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#a-roadmap-with-actual-content).

**Examples**

> Chapter 2 reviews related work in human-robot interaction, robot manipulation, and multi-party interaction modeling, and describes early work with children that motivates the utility of moderated group interactions with robots.
>
> — [Short, p. 8 (PDF page 26)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=26)

> Our results suggest that the proposed framework can help better detect non-interacting people in social environments, like bystanders, without sacrificing group detection performance.
>
> — [Vázquez, p. 5 (PDF page 12)](../dessertation_reference/Vazquez_2017_CMU_thesis.pdf#page=12)

> In this first part, we present a unifying lens on aligning robot and human representations that serves as the foundation on top of which the remaining three parts of the thesis are built.
>
> — [Bobu, p. 3 (PDF page 17)](../dessertation_reference/Bobu_2023_Berkeley_EECS-2023-200.pdf#page=17)

[All 4 source examples for this pattern](source-evidence.md#an-outline-with-content).

### Chapter openings

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

A chapter opening tells the reader what the chapter contains and where it sits. Short begins each chapter before the last with a short abstract in italics that says what the chapter presents and, for a study chapter, what was found. A paragraph that places the chapter after the earlier ones follows. Vázquez begins with what the chapter presents or with what the previous experiments left open. Bobu begins with the publication note and a figure, then a scene, the problem with existing methods, her argument, and the contributions of the chapter.

**Reusable forms**

- This chapter introduces [method], in which [one clause]. [Method] is then [instantiated / evaluated] in [study]. The results show that [finding].
- In the [earlier studies], [what was the case]. This chapter examines [what follows from it].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Hang's study chapters open with a summary of the study and its findings, followed by where the related work is reviewed and, for the published chapters, where the work appeared. That is Short's opening. The summary states the finding and does not only announce the topic.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [A roadmap with actual content](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#a-roadmap-with-actual-content) and [The three stage dissertation arc](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#the-three-stage-dissertation-arc).

**Examples**

> This chapter presents the third moderation algorithm, which directly models the social relationships between users as a directed graph and takes actions to affect those relationships.
>
> — [Short, p. 136 (PDF page 154)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=154)

> In the first two experiments presented in Chapter 5, the orientation of the robot was manually controlled by a wizard during the whole interaction.
>
> — [Vázquez, p. 53 (PDF page 60)](../dessertation_reference/Vazquez_2017_CMU_thesis.pdf#page=60)

> Just like in Chapter 3, we are motivated here by learning representations that lead to learning generalizable rewards.
>
> — [Bobu, p. 98 (PDF page 112)](../dessertation_reference/Bobu_2023_Berkeley_EECS-2023-200.pdf#page=112)

### Linking a chapter to the one before

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

The first paragraphs of a chapter say what the previous chapter established and what this one adds. Short writes that the results presented in the previous chapter support a claim and that this chapter builds on those results. Vázquez writes that a limitation or a success in the last experiment motivated the next one. Bobu writes what has been assumed so far and why that is not always enough, or that now that one thing has been tested, the next is analyzed.

**Reusable forms**

- The results presented in [chapter] support [claim]. This chapter builds on those results to [purpose].
- This limitation motivated [the work in this chapter].
- So far, [assumption]. [Why it does not always hold]. In [chapter], [what is done about it].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Same as the handoff in Hang's reference, and Hang's chapter connections already follow Short. Keep the boundary that the studies used different tasks and participants, so the link is a motivation and not a single pipeline.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [A study finding leading to a method](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#a-study-finding-leading-to-a-method) and [The three stage dissertation arc](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#the-three-stage-dissertation-arc).

**Examples**

> The results presented in Chapter 5 support the use of a socially assistive robot moderator in group interactions and indicate that such a moderator may be able to improve the interaction, even without the use of speech understanding.
>
> — [Short, p. 117 (PDF page 135)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=135)

> The success of the orientation behaviors that we studied in our last experiment motivated us to explore reinforcement learning techniques to generate socially appropriate orientation behaviors for robots during multi-party conversations.

> — [Vázquez, p. 5 (PDF page 12)](../dessertation_reference/Vazquez_2017_CMU_thesis.pdf#page=12)

> Now that we have tested our method for learning features with both experts and non-experts, we analyze how the learned features affect reward learning.

> — [Bobu, p. 42 (PDF page 56)](../dessertation_reference/Bobu_2023_Berkeley_EECS-2023-200.pdf#page=56)

### Chapter endings

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

A chapter ends by saying what it showed and what that provides for the rest of the dissertation. Short ends each chapter after the introduction with a section titled Summary, written in the past tense, that restates the method, the study, and the result, and closes with what the results support. Vázquez ends the long study chapter with a Summary whose last sentence points to the next chapter, and two later chapters with a Discussion. Bobu ends each chapter with a Discussion that restates the proposal and gives implications and limitations, and it often closes with a sentence that begins with Overall.

**Reusable forms**

- This chapter presented [content]. [Method] was [instantiated / evaluated] in [study]. [Result]. These results support [claim] and provide a basis for [next step].
- The next chapter describes [what follows].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Hang's study chapters end with a Discussion and a Summary, which is Short's structure. Hang's reference says to end with a link to the next chapter only when that chapter addresses the named question.

The related pattern in Hang's reference is [Discussion synthesis and its boundary](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#discussion-synthesis-and-its-boundary).

**Examples**

> This chapter presented four studies of human-robot interaction that inform the development of the robot controllers used in this dissertation.

> — [Short, p. 99 (PDF page 117)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=117)

> The next chapter describes our efforts in this direction.

> — [Vázquez, p. 38 (PDF page 45)](../dessertation_reference/Vazquez_2017_CMU_thesis.pdf#page=45)

> In this work, we propose that robots can learn more generalizable rewards by using a divide-and-conquer approach, focusing on learning features separately from learning how to combine them.
>

### A study in a fixed order

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

Every study is reported in the same order, so a reader can find the same information in the same place. Short uses Methodology, Results, and Discussion, with research questions or hypotheses first, then design, setup, measures, and participants, and results grouped by measure. Vázquez uses Method, Results, and Discussion with Participants and Procedure as subsections. Bobu uses run-in labels in a fixed sequence, which are Manipulated Variables, Dependent Measures, Hypotheses, and Participants, followed by an analysis divided into objective and subjective results and a short Summary.

**Reusable forms**

- The study addressed [N] research questions, as follows.
- Participants. [N, recruitment, sample properties]. Hypotheses. H[n]. [Statement].
- This section presents the results of [study] according to the measures described in [section].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Hang's study chapters all end with a Discussion and a Summary. The sections before them have different names in different chapters, such as Study Design and Experimental Setup in one and Methodology in others. Each of the three keeps one order and one set of headings for every study. Whether to align the headings is Hang's decision. Hang's reference handles each results paragraph.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [A results paragraph answering one comparison](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#a-results-paragraph-answering-one-comparison) and [Study actions leading to the analytical question](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#study-actions-leading-to-the-analytical-question).

**Examples**

> Our study addressed five research questions, as follows:
>
> — [Short, p. 43 (PDF page 61)](../dessertation_reference/Short_2017_USC_thesis.pdf#page=61)

> Participants only experienced one of two conditions: without sidekick (C) or sidekick (S).
>
> — [Vázquez, p. 20 (PDF page 27)](../dessertation_reference/Vazquez_2017_CMU_thesis.pdf#page=27)

> Hypotheses. H4: FERL learns good features from non-expert user data.
>
> — [Bobu, p. 40 (PDF page 54)](../dessertation_reference/Bobu_2023_Berkeley_EECS-2023-200.pdf#page=54)

### A method from idea to steps

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

A method is explained in words before it is given in symbols or as an algorithm. Short describes the model as a cycle of monitoring, evaluating, and acting, defines its terms, and only then gives the algorithm and one instantiation. Vázquez states the problem and its assumptions, gives the core of the framework in one sentence, and walks through the steps with First and Then. Bobu gives the intuition, says why the direct approach is difficult, and then says what is done instead.

**Reusable forms**

- At the core of [framework] is [procedure]. First, [step]. Then, [step].
- Since [direct approach] is difficult, [method] instead [operation].
- Throughout [process], [agent] [monitors / computes] [quantity], taking [action] at [interval or condition].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Same as Hang's reference, which explains the meaning of a signal and the intuition before notation and losses. A method name is the subject of its own operations.

Related patterns in Hang's reference are [Teaching a method from meaning to evaluation](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#teaching-a-method-from-meaning-to-evaluation), [Explaining the mechanism](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#explaining-the-mechanism), and [An intuition leading to an operation](../writing_style_ref/hangs-writing-style-reference.md#an-intuition-leading-to-an-operation).

**Examples**

> Our intuition is that if a human judges two behaviors to be similar, then their representations should also be similar.

### Related work that ends on this work

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

A passage of related work closes by placing the dissertation next to what was just reviewed. Short says what the dissertation leverages from a field and what it contributes to it, and how her robot differs from similar ones. Vázquez ends a group of studies with what remains unclear and with our efforts build upon this work. Bobu ends each group with By contrast or We instead and the specific difference. The three place related work differently. Short has one background chapter, Vázquez has a background chapter, a related work chapter, and short sections before each experiment, and Bobu has a Prior Work section in each chapter.

**Reusable forms**

- This dissertation leverages results in [field] about [topic], and contributes to this area by [contribution].
- It is unclear from this work whether [open question].
- Prior work [approach]. This chapter instead [approach], rather than [alternative].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Same as Hang's reference. Hang's structure is Short's, with one Background chapter that each study chapter points to. A difference is not by itself an advantage.

### A discussion in themed paragraphs

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

A discussion is divided by theme, and each theme is visible at the start of its paragraph. Short uses a run-in heading that ends with a colon. Vázquez uses a lead sentence in small capitals. Bobu uses a run-in heading that ends with a period, such as Implications for a setting and Limitations and Future Work. The themes cover what was found, what it means for design, what limits it, and what comes next.

**Reusable forms**

- [Theme as a noun phrase]. [Paragraph].
- [Finding as a short sentence]. [Paragraph with the evidence].
- Limitations and Future Work. [Paragraph].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Same as Hang's reference, which gives discussion run-in headings a concrete subject. Short's headings end with a colon. A period after the heading, as in Bobu, follows Hang's preference for fewer colons.

### Discussion order

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Hang's reference says the same.*

A study discussion begins by restating the goal or what was done, in one or two sentences. It then reports which hypotheses were supported, explains the results that went against expectation, states the limits of the study, and ends with what the study provides for the following work. Short and Vázquez follow this order closely. Bobu begins with the proposal and the main evidence and then gives implications before limitations.

**Reusable forms**

- The goal of this study was to examine [question].
- [What was done, in one sentence]. [What was found]. [What was not found, with a possible reason]. [Limits]. [What this provides for the next chapter].

**In Hang's dissertation.** Same as the discussion pattern in Hang's reference, which moves from connection to evidence, implication, boundary, and next question. ### Future work from scope and limits

Future work is derived from the choices and limits of the dissertation. Vázquez titles the section Limitations and Avenues for Future Research and pairs each limitation with an avenue. Bobu says that the dissertation has made progress and that it opens further directions, and groups them under three headings. Each direction starts from what the dissertation did and says what would change.

**Reusable forms**

- A number of choices were made in scoping this dissertation, and each provides a starting point for future work.

### The final chapter

*Seen in Short, Vázquez, and Bobu. Adds to Hang's reference.*

The final chapters are short, at three to four pages each. Bobu's is titled Conclusion and Future Work and has a recap of her position, the limitation that leads to a broader question, and three groups of directions. **Reusable forms**

**In Hang's dissertation.** Hang's dissertation has an Overall Discussion chapter before the Conclusion, which goes beyond what the three did and is Hang's own structure. ## Scope of this reference

The sources are three dissertations. The abstract, the introduction, and the final chapter of each were read closely, together with the openings, results, discussions, and summaries of selected chapters. The remaining text was searched for the patterns found there. A pattern marked as seen in a writer has at least one checked quotation from that writer, and a writer who is not named may still use the pattern in a part that was not read.

Chapters of all three dissertations were first published as papers with coauthors. The patterns describe the dissertations and do not attribute each sentence to its named author. The measures are counts on text extracted from the PDF files, with figures, tables, equations, and reference lists left out as far as the extraction allows. They show differences of degree and are not targets.

The lines headed In Hang's dissertation are readings made when this reference was prepared, on October 12, 2026, from Hang's reference and from the dissertation files of that day. They are not Hang's decisions, and a later instruction from Hang replaces them. This reference changes no dissertation text.
