# Portable writing style guide

A compact guide to Hang Yu's writing style for research papers, theses, and talks in robot learning and human-robot interaction. It is written to apply to any paper, and it needs no other files.

| File | Use |
| --- | --- |
| [writing-style-guide.md](writing-style-guide.md) | Start here. Core philosophy, which guidance wins, a quick reference, and the rules by kind, each with short examples of wording that was replaced or kept |
| [vocabulary.md](vocabulary.md) | Terms with fixed meanings, the author's technical and research-writing vocabulary by familiarity, the vocabulary of five field dissertations, words to watch, and acronyms |
| [field-conventions.md](field-conventions.md) | What five robotics and HRI dissertations do, with measures, where the writers differ, and conventions for structuring a long document |

## How to use it

Before drafting, read the core philosophy and the quick reference. When revising, look up the section for the kind of sentence or passage at hand. Check unfamiliar terms in the vocabulary, and define any term the author has only read. For the structure of a thesis or a long paper, see the field conventions.

When guidance conflicts, the author's current instructions come first, then the facts and definitions of the passage, then the author's past choices, then the style guide, and last the field conventions.

To give the guide to an AI assistant, attach the three files and ask it to follow writing-style-guide.md, with vocabulary.md for word choice and field-conventions.md for structure.

## Where it comes from

Condensed on November 6, 2026 from two larger references in Hang's dissertation folder. One is `writing_style_ref`, a reference of 72 patterns built from six published papers and from the author's instructions, hand edits, and decisions in 16 reviews, with a vocabulary of 929 terms. The other is `committee_style_ref`, a reference of 65 patterns and a vocabulary of 515 terms built from the dissertations of Short, Vázquez, Bobu, Matarić, and Scassellati.

The condensed version keeps the rules, the vocabulary, short examples of replaced or declined wording, and the core philosophy. It leaves out the extracted source texts, the quotations from papers and dissertations, the records of each review and each decision, the evidence weights, the counts and example sentences of the vocabulary, the full word-family lists, the validation reports, and the build and check scripts. Rules that applied only to one dissertation were rewritten to apply to any paper or thesis.
